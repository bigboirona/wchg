01 / The protection checkup

Three useful questions
to answer first.

You do not need an exact coverage amount or a product choice to begin. These are the facts to put on the table.

01

Who depends
on your income?

Think about children, a spouse or partner, housing, debts, education goals and caregiving. Include the responsibilities you expect to carry in the years ahead.

02

What coverage
already exists?

Workplace benefits and personal policies belong in the same review. Ask what they provide, how long they last and whether workplace coverage follows you when employment changes.

03

How long is
protection needed?

A mortgage, a child's education and a business obligation can have different timelines. Knowing those dates helps frame the coverage and premium questions.

02 / General education

Different needs.
Different tradeoffs.

Term and permanent coverage address different durations, premiums and policy features.

Start with the protection objective, then discuss what the policy requires and how it fits your timeline and budget. A useful review does not assume a particular carrier or policy is right for you.

03 / Before a conversation

A few details
to have in mind.

Keep your initial call or message general. Sensitive records belong in an appropriate secure workflow.

What should I be ready to discuss?

Who relies on you, the obligations you want to protect, any coverage you already have and the question that brought you here. A general premium budget and time horizon can help make the conversation more useful.

Do I need to choose a type of policy first?

No. Start with the responsibility or planning problem. Coverage duration, policy features, premium commitments and tradeoffs can be discussed before considering a particular carrier or policy.

Can the website confirm coverage or eligibility?

No. This site offers education and direct contact with Derek. Availability and eligibility depend on your state and individual circumstances; a licensed review and the carrier's underwriting process are separate next steps. Coverage is not bound here.

What should I leave out of a text or email?

Do not send Social Security numbers, payment-card information, medical records or other highly sensitive details in an ordinary text or email. Begin with the general question and preferred way to speak.
