01 / Coverage basics

Begin with the
planning objective.

Protection, cash value flexibility, business continuity and legacy liquidity can lead to different policy designs.

The first conversation considers who or what needs protection, for how long, your existing coverage and how much premium flexibility you need.

A defined period

Term life insurance

Term coverage provides protection for a specified period. It generally has no cash value. Ask about the term length, premium schedule, renewal and any conversion provisions.

Longer-term design

Permanent life insurance

Permanent policies combine a death benefit with a cash value component. Whole life, universal life and IUL have different structures; ongoing coverage depends on the policy terms and required funding.

This is general education, not a recommendation of a specific carrier or policy. A complete licensed review considers your circumstances and the actual contract.

02 / IUL, cash value & premium financing

Look closely at
the moving parts.

Indexed universal life and premium-financed planning need more than an optimistic illustration.

  • Policy structure. Understand the death benefit, policy costs, crediting methods, caps and participation rates.
  • Funding. Review planned premiums, overfunded policy design concepts and what happens if funding changes.
  • Loans. Discuss loan assumptions, liquidity, repayment and the effect on the policy.
  • Financing. Examine interest-rate exposure, collateral requirements and how a leveraged strategy would be monitored.

Keep the risks beside the idea

Projected values are not promises.

IUL is life insurance, not a direct investment in an index. Non-guaranteed values can change. Policy costs, caps, participation rates, loan rates and ongoing funding affect results.

Policy loans, leverage and premium financing can create risk if the policy is underfunded, loan rates rise, collateral requirements change or the strategy is not monitored. Financing also adds repayment obligations.

Any strategy should be reviewed with carrier illustrations and appropriate tax, legal and financial professionals.

03 / Questions worth asking

Read beyond
the headline number.

Use these questions to prepare for a policy discussion. Open any topic for more detail.

What is guaranteed, and what is illustrated?

Ask which values and benefits are guaranteed by the contract and which rely on assumptions that can change. Review the guaranteed and non-guaranteed columns, not just the illustrated outcome.

What happens if I change the premiums?

Ask how reduced, delayed or discontinued funding would affect coverage, policy values and the premiums needed later. Premium flexibility should be reviewed alongside the policy costs and funding requirements.

How should policy loans be reviewed?

Review the loan interest rate, how loans affect policy values and benefits, and what happens as the balance grows. Ask for scenarios with different loan and crediting assumptions and a clear discussion of repayment and lapse risk.

What if I already have a policy?

Begin with the existing contract and a current policy statement or illustration. Understand its coverage, premiums, costs and provisions before considering a change. A new illustration alone is not a reason to replace coverage.

What about business, legacy or annuity questions?

Key person protection, buy-sell funding, estate liquidity, beneficiary planning and retirement-income questions begin with different objectives. Coordinate the insurance discussion with your appropriate legal, tax and financial professionals.
